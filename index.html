What Should You Do After a Car Accident?

Your health should be your first priority following a motor vehicle accident. Even when a collision appears minor, physical symptoms may develop gradually.

Consider these steps:

  • Check for immediate danger: If anyone has serious injuries, call 911. Do not move someone who may have a neck or spinal injury unless there is an immediate danger.
  • Seek medical attention: Get evaluated promptly if you experience pain, dizziness, bruising, stiffness, or other symptoms.
  • Monitor your symptoms: Pay attention to new or worsening discomfort, headaches, weakness, or changes in movement.
  • Keep relevant medical information: Record your symptoms, medical visits, test results, and treatment recommendations.
  • Follow medical advice: Attend recommended follow-up appointments and seek additional care if symptoms change.
Important: Do not delay emergency care to arrange an urgent care appointment or collect insurance information.

Can Urgent Care Evaluate Car Accident Injuries?

Yes. Urgent care clinics may evaluate certain stable, non-life-threatening injuries following a car accident.

Depending on the patient's symptoms and the clinic's capabilities, a post-accident injury evaluation may include a medical history, physical examination, assessment of pain and movement, and diagnostic imaging when appropriate.

Urgent care may be suitable for concerns such as:

  • Mild muscle strains.
  • Minor sprains.
  • Stable joint pain.
  • Minor cuts and abrasions.
  • Localized bruising.
  • Certain uncomplicated injuries that require medical assessment.

However, urgent care is not designed to replace emergency departments or trauma centers. Major collisions, suspected internal injuries, serious head injuries, spinal injuries, and severe fractures require emergency evaluation.

Remember: If you are uncertain about the seriousness of an injury, seek emergency medical advice rather than assuming urgent care is sufficient.

Common Car Accident Injuries That May Need Medical Evaluation

A collision can affect different parts of the body. Some injuries are visible immediately, while others become noticeable over time.

1. Whiplash and Neck Pain

Whiplash is a neck injury that can occur when the head moves suddenly forward and backward during a collision.

Possible symptoms include:

  • Neck stiffness.
  • Neck pain.
  • Headaches.
  • Shoulder discomfort.
  • Reduced neck movement.
  • Pain extending toward the arms.

Symptoms may not appear immediately. MedlinePlus explains that whiplash discomfort can develop hours or even weeks after an accident.

A healthcare professional can evaluate symptoms and determine whether further examination or imaging is needed.

2. Back Pain and Muscle Strains

The force of a collision can strain muscles and other soft tissues in the back.

Patients may experience:

  • Lower back discomfort.
  • Muscle tightness.
  • Pain when bending.
  • Stiffness while sitting or standing.
  • Difficulty performing everyday activities.
Severe back pain, leg weakness, numbness, or loss of bladder or bowel control requires emergency evaluation.

3. Sprains and Joint Injuries

A sudden impact can place stress on joints, ligaments, and surrounding tissues.

Common symptoms include swelling, tenderness, bruising, and restricted movement.

A healthcare provider can assess the affected area and determine whether an on-site X-ray or additional evaluation is appropriate.

4. Fractures and Broken Bones

Some car accidents result in fractures involving the wrists, hands, arms, legs, ribs, or other bones.

Possible warning signs include:

  • Significant swelling.
  • Severe localized pain.
  • Visible deformity.
  • Difficulty moving the affected area.
  • Inability to put weight on an injured limb.

An X-ray may help identify certain fractures. Suspected severe or complicated fractures should be evaluated in an emergency department.

5. Head Injuries and Concussion Symptoms

A collision can cause the head to strike an object or move suddenly during impact.

Possible concussion symptoms include:

  • Headache.
  • Dizziness.
  • Nausea.
  • Sensitivity to light or noise.
  • Difficulty concentrating.
  • Memory problems.
  • Feeling unusually tired.

Concussion symptoms can develop gradually. A suspected concussion should be assessed by a healthcare professional.

Seek emergency care immediately for worsening headaches, repeated vomiting, seizures, increasing confusion, weakness, slurred speech, loss of consciousness, or difficulty waking.

When Should You Go to the Emergency Room After a Car Accident?

Recognizing emergency warning signs is essential when choosing between urgent care and the emergency department.

Call 911 or seek emergency medical attention immediately if you experience:

  • Loss of consciousness or fainting.
  • Severe headache following a head injury.
  • Repeated vomiting after a head injury.
  • Severe chest pain or difficulty breathing.
  • Heavy or uncontrolled bleeding.
  • Severe abdominal pain or suspected internal injury.
  • Neck or spinal injury with numbness, weakness, or loss of movement.
  • Seizures or sudden confusion.
  • A serious or visibly deformed fracture.

These symptoms can indicate potentially life-threatening injuries that require emergency evaluation.

Emergency warning: Do not drive yourself if you have serious symptoms. Call 911.

Car Accident X-Ray: When Is Imaging Necessary?

A car accident X-ray may be recommended when a healthcare professional suspects a fracture or another injury that can be evaluated through radiographic imaging.

X-rays use a small amount of radiation to produce images of internal body structures, particularly bones.

What Can an X-Ray Help Identify?

Depending on the body area examined, an X-ray may help identify:

  • Certain bone fractures.
  • Joint dislocations.
  • Bone alignment abnormalities.
  • Some chest injuries.

However, an X-ray cannot identify every type of car accident injury.

For example, muscle strains, ligament injuries, many soft tissue injuries, and concussions cannot be ruled out by a normal X-ray. Additional examinations or imaging may be necessary depending on the clinical findings.

Do You Need an X-Ray After a Minor Car Accident?

Not everyone involved in a collision needs an X-ray.

A healthcare professional considers factors such as:

  • Where the pain is located.
  • Whether swelling or tenderness is present.
  • The patient's ability to move the affected area.
  • The nature of the collision.
  • Physical examination findings.
  • Whether a fracture or other structural injury is suspected.

The decision to order imaging should be based on medical necessity rather than the severity of visible vehicle damage alone.
